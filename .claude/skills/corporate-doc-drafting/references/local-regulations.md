## 1. Governing principles

- A new local regulation **shall not conflict** with previously approved documents. To change earlier
  requirements, those documents are cancelled or newly approved in full compliance with the standard.
- **Each local regulation is approved by a separate Order.** The Order's recital states the grounds
  for preparation; the ordering section opens with `I HEREBY ORDER…`; Clause 1 reads
  `To approve…` followed by the regulation title. Where prior documents are superseded, Clause 2
  begins `To consider… null and void` and lists every cancelled regulation with the reference details
  of its approving Order.
- Copy-pasting provisions from external documents **without adaptation to the entity's specifics is
  not permitted**.

## 2. Mandatory structure

In this exact order:

1. Title Sheet
2. Revision history log
3. Table of Contents
4. General Provisions
5. Reference regulatory documents
6. Terms, definitions and abbreviations
7. Body of the text
8. Attachments

Flowcharts describing the relevant processes are placed in the attachments, where applicable.

## 3. Title, Table of Contents, Revision History Log

**Title** — the first word states the document type and is typed in **CAPITAL LETTERS**; remaining
words in lowercase. E.g. `GUIDELINES for Documentation Support of Management Activities`.

**Table of Contents** — lists all paragraphs, sections, subsections and attachments with page
numbers. Attachments are listed at the end. Left-aligned, with **clickable links** to the sections.

**Revision History Log** — describes every change in every revision. Rev 00 carries the note
"First Issue".

| Rev. | Date issued | Section | Change description |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00 | dd.mm.yyyy | General | First Issue |
| 01 | dd.mm.yyyy | 6.1.2 | State departments' wording and reference added |

Log entries at section granularity, not document granularity — the value of the log is that a reader
can find exactly what moved. A full rewrite is logged as `General — Document fully revised, all
sections updated.`

## 4. General Provisions

Must contain:
- Brief definition of the issue and purpose regulated by the document;
- Type of document (corporate management standard, corporate functional standard, or enterprise
  standard) and the related business process;
- Reference to the document(s) forming the basis for development;
- Area of application (coverage and intended users);
- Terms and definitions essential for understanding.

State the business process explicitly, e.g. "refers to business process 03 – Corporate governance".
Also state what the document does **not** cover — exclusions prevent scope disputes later.

## 5. Body, headings, formulas, tables, references

**Body** — brief, accurate, logically consistent, and sufficient for application. Contains the
General Provisions plus a detailed description of the sequence, methods and procedures of
implementing the business processes.

**Headings** — bold, left-aligned, no full stop. Subparagraphs numbered continuously:
```
1. General provisions
   1.1. Objective
   1.2. Type
```

**Formulas** — numbered consecutively with Arabic numerals in parentheses; references to formulas
given in parentheses. Symbols and coefficients defined immediately below the formula unless already
defined:
```
where:
DCFt – discounted cash flow of the year t;
CFt  – cash flow of year t;
DFt  – discounting factor;
RD   – discounting rate;
t    – serial number of the modeling interval (year).
```

**Tables** — title reflects the content and is **centred above** the table. A continuing table shows
its title only once, with `Continued from previous page` or `End of table` on subsequent pages.
Column headings repeat on every page; where columns are too wide, headings may be replaced by column
numbers in Arabic numerals. Narrow tables may be arranged side by side on one page.

**Graphical materials** — titles placed **below** the material.

**References** — cite paragraph and subparagraph numbers; for graphical or tabular attachments,
references to specific columns/rows are allowed. Acceptable wording: "in accordance with …",
"according to …". The References section lists every referenced document; external documents are
cited by **full title**.

## 6. Attachments

- Contain tables, graphical materials (pictures, schemes, charts, flowcharts), or unified document
  forms — both mandatory and recommended.
- The text must reference **every** attachment, with its number indicated.
- Attachments establishing mandatory unified forms are titled with the official document name
  (e.g. `Annual Plan of Capital Investments`).
- Attachments provided as samples are headed with the word `Sample` followed by the document title.
- Page numbers of attachments are indicated **starting from the second page**.

## 7. Explanatory note and Business Process Model

The Executor prepares an **explanatory note** (maximum 2 pages when submitted for approval)
containing:
- Rationale for development or update of the regulation;
- Subject of regulation;
- Concept of the draft regulation;
- Description of organisational, technical, financial and other measures resulting from adoption;
- The role and functions of the Managing Director under the document;
- Where it is a revision: a "what was / what is" summary of changes.

The Executor also prepares a **Business Process Model** which must comply with the Group's Business
Process Management System modelling requirements and describe the process per the Business Process
Register — roles, results, KPIs, consumers, and suppliers of source data.

## 8. Risk and Controls Matrix (RCM)

**All regulations shall include a Risk and Controls Matrix.** It is issued as an attachment to any
regulatory document defining procedures, standards, requirements, norms, regulations or
methodologies for business processes.

- The **document author** determines the presence of risks and prepares the matrix. Document Control
  does **not** fill it in — it only verifies its presence.
- **Documents lacking a Risk and Controls Matrix shall not be accepted for review and approval.**
  Treat a missing RCM as a hard stop.
- The completed matrix is attached to the **PDF version as the final pages**.
- The matrix may carry a date different from that of the order.
- Responsibility for providing completed matrices rests with the document owner.

**Column scheme:**

| Columns | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Sequential numbering of records |
| 2–5 | Objectives and processes (operations/transactions) necessary to achieve the objectives, linked to the relevant items of the regulatory document |
| 6–7 | Controlled risks — numbering plus brief descriptions of possible adverse events affecting process objectives |
| 8–9 | Control procedures designed to mitigate the risks, described clearly, cross-referenced to the regulatory document |
| 2, 4, 6, 8 | Unique numbering (codes) for objectives, processes, risks and control procedures |

Where multiple risks or controls apply to the same objective or process, they are listed **under the
same code**.

The matrix may additionally record: likelihood of occurrence; impact magnitude; risk mitigation
measures; persons responsible for control and management.

The design intent: every risk must trace back to a numbered clause of the regulation, and every
control must trace forward to a named owner. A matrix whose rows cannot be tied to specific clauses
has failed its purpose.

## 9. Operations Technical Documentation (OTD)

OTD is a **type of Local Regulation** applied to operational business processes. It is regulatory in
nature and subject to all general requirements — **including the Risk and Controls Matrix** — but
follows a distinct review and approval workflow because of its revision-based nature and integration
with technical systems.

Typical covered processes: innovation, research and development management; health, safety and
environment management; information technology and data management; well construction, workover and
completion management; energy supply, saving and efficiency; production, maintenance and logistics
management.

### Review and approval — in the technical DMS (IDMS)

- OTD of the 2nd, 3rd, 4th and 5th business-process levels is reviewed, approved and stored in the
  IDMS, and approved by the respective **Discipline Line Managers**.
- The Initiator determines the business process against the Asset's Business Process Register
  **before** drafting.
- Reviewers, endorsers and approvers are defined in the **Document Distribution Matrix (DDM)**. The
  Document Owner is **solely responsible** for the DDM's accuracy and completeness and for its timely
  submission to Document Control. If a document is not listed in the DDM, the Owner updates the
  matrix and submits the revised version.
- **The Initiator may not close their IDMS assignment until all mandatory approvers have completed
  their review.**

Role definitions worth preserving in any DDM:
- **Reviewer** — authorised to review and comment.
- **Endorser** — reviews, restarts review, creates and edits the comments sheet.
- **Approver** — responsible for approval of the document.

### Formal release — via the administrative DMS (ESDO)

Because OTD is fully approved in the IDMS, **no additional approval is required** in the
administrative DMS. But every Local Regulation must still be issued by an official Order.

Package submitted to Document Control:
1. Draft Order
2. Explanatory note
3. Risks and Controls Matrix (incorporated in the OTD)
4. Attachments, if applicable
5. Distribution list
6. IDMS Approval Report

Document Control forwards the package to the Director; on approval it registers the Order and
distributes per the distribution list.

**Hard rules:**
- A single Order covers **one document and one revision only**.
- Orders may **not** be issued for multiple OTD documents simultaneously.
- The **Chief Engineer** defines which OTD requires release by official Order.

### Responsibilities

| Role | Accountability |
|---|---|
| Document Owner | Formatting compliance, DDM accuracy, Order package preparation |
| Discipline Line Managers | Mandatory approvers for OTD in their areas |
| Chief Engineer | Defines the list of OTD subject to release by Order |
| Document Control | Verifies package completeness, submits for Director's approval, registers, distributes |
| Director | Approves Orders on the basis of IDMS review results |
