## 1. Registration

**Non-negotiables:**
- All incoming and outgoing correspondence is subject to mandatory registration.
- A document may be registered **only once**.
- Only registered documents are official. No registration number = not official.
- **Altering a signed and registered document is prohibited.**
- Responsibility for timely submission rests with the Document Owner and Initiator.
- All official incoming correspondence is submitted to Document Control on the **day of receipt**;
  where it lands in a personal mailbox, by **18:00 the same day**.

**Registration request forms** carry the minimum card data: company code (dropdown); subject
(manual, identical to the letter's subject line); sender name; addressee; reference number if
applicable; additional comments; and a checkbox for whether the document requires a resolution.

**Incoming electronic** must be: on the sender's official letterhead in PDF; signed by an authorised
representative; carrying reference number, sending date, addressee and subject line; with all
enclosures listed in the body text. Size limits: 10 MB (external mailboxes), 20 MB (corporate).
Oversized files go on external media or as RAR/ZIP volumes. Document Control verifies format
compliance, attachment completeness, and readability, and notifies the sender immediately of
discrepancies.

**Incoming paper** — used where wet signatures or stamps are required (court and government
documents, contracts, tender packages). Packages marked "Personal", "Confidential", "Commercial
secret" or "Tender proposals" are opened **only by the recipient**. Damaged package integrity is
noted on the receipt in the courier's presence, with photo evidence sent to the sender. Envelopes are
retained where the matter is legal (court, contract disputes, claims) or where the sender or sending
date is missing; otherwise they may be disposed of. Paper handed directly to an employee is scanned
at **300 dpi**, properly oriented and legible, then forwarded for registration.

**Registration ownership by document class:**

| Class | Registered by |
|---|---|
| ORD on principal activity | Document Control |
| Orders on personnel | Document Control, then transferred to HR for safekeeping |
| Minutes of meetings attended by the Director | Document Control |
| Other minutes | Designated Responsible Executor |
| Internal documents for approval (plans, programs, reports, registers) | Functional manager or committee |
| Legal documents (contracts, agreements, obligations) | Legal Affairs (letters of commitment: Document Control) |
| Financial and accounting documents | Accounting, per Accounting Policy |
| Tender documentation | Contracts & Procurement |
| Personnel documents | HR (except Orders on personnel) |

Organizational and regulatory documents, including Local Regulations, **take effect on the date of
their execution** unless another effective date is stated in the text.

**Business unit obligations:** each manager appoints an employee responsible for document
management — receiving and distributing documents, routing for execution, monitoring status, filing
executed documents, maintaining the register, and delivering to Document Control. Misrouted
correspondence is returned with a note. Employees leaving for vacation, business trip or on
termination must transfer all assigned documents to a replacement or line manager.

## 2. Resolutions vs Assignments

The Director issues two distinct instruments on incoming correspondence — do not conflate them:

| | **Resolution** | **Assignment** |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Decision or instruction with **no** execution timeline or specific action requirement | Decision or instruction imposing **defined timelines and specific actions** |
| Typical wording | "For action", "For information", "To take into account", "For reference", "For consideration" | Named action, named executor, named deadline |
| Lifecycle | Archived, distributed for information, valid until revoked | Tracked under control until closed |

**Where several Executors are named, the person or unit listed FIRST is the Responsible Executor;
all others are Co-Executors.** Co-Executors are equally responsible for timely, high-quality
execution; the Responsible Executor has the right to control their actions. A document is fulfilled
only when **all** listed tasks are completed.

If the head of a business unit is replaced and the duties remain the same, control over assignments
transfers automatically to the newly appointed head.

## 3. Execution deadlines and delegation

Deadlines run from the date of **signing/approval** for internal documents (MOMs, MEMOs) and from the
date of **registration** for documents from external organisations.

**Standard deadlines where none is specified:**

| Document | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Minutes of meetings, peer review meetings | 30 days |
| NOC letters | 7 days |
| Parent company letters | 7 days |
| Urgent documents | 3 days |
| Draft responses and letters | 3 days |

**Special rules:**
- Where the document and the resolution give different deadlines, the **resolution prevails**.
- "Within a month" / "within a year" → the execution date is the **first day of the following
  month/year**.
- Long-term assignments require **intermediate control dates**.
- Extension requires approval by the Director or the Director's Personal Assistant, with
  justification and a proposed new due date; the approval is forwarded to Document Control. Requests
  without it are not accepted for closure.
- Closure of an assignment deemed no longer relevant requires the same approval, with justification.
- **Assignments must be executed regardless of off-shift periods or business trips.** A rotational
  employee with a deadline falling after shift end must delegate to their back-to-back and record it
  in the handover.
- A Responsible Executor may involve other employees but **cannot delegate an assignment** without
  approval; deadlines remain unchanged. Reassignment is coordinated directly with the Director or PA,
  with Document Control notified.
- When a Responsible Executor leaves the position, the newly appointed person automatically assumes
  all active assignments.

## 4. Monitoring, progress comments and closure

**Reporting cadence:** Document Control issues a report of all active assignments to Responsible
Executors every **Thursday**, and a report to the Director (copying the PA) every **Saturday**.
Executors may self-monitor via the DMS resolutions report. Automatic reminders can be configured for
3, 2 and 1 day before the due date and on the due date.

**Progress comments** must be concise and factual, describing the actual work performed.

- Unacceptable: "completed", "in progress", "at the stage of completion".
- Acceptable: `Draft reply prepared; legal review requested on 18.10`.

**An assignment is deemed NOT executed if** the response contains only proposals or intermediate
actions, or if no timely execution information is provided.

**Closure.** Documents are removed from control only after full execution. Grounds:
- Requiring a written response → an official letter or office memorandum.
- Not requiring a written response → an e-mail, recorded in writing by Document Control.

Process: the Responsible Executor obtains the Director's or PA's approval of the execution results →
forwards the approval with supporting documents to Document Control → Document Control archives all
approvals in the DMS and on the corporate server with the suffix `_assignment completion
acknowledgement`. An assignment on an incoming letter closes by default once the outgoing response
is sent. **Intermediate or preliminary responses do not constitute completion.**

Document Control closes assignments with the status **"completed without overdue"** or
**"completed with overdue"** — the distinction is retained deliberately for performance analysis.

## 5. Approval of draft documents

Approval confirms advisability, justification, and compliance with legislation and the entity's own
regulatory documents. Internal approval runs through the DMS route; external approval may use an
approval field, approval reference, approval sheet, or attached minutes of the discussion.

Submission is initiated by the **Business Process Owner (not lower than Functional Manager level)**.
Before submission, the Responsible Executor discusses the draft with all stakeholders and prepares a
brief explanatory note (maximum 2 pages) covering goal, contents, and — for revisions — a
"what was / what is" comparison.

### Draft ORD package

| # | Document | Format |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explanatory note | PDF, signed by Supervisor |
| 2 | Draft order | DOC |
| 3 | Attachment to the order (Procedure / Guidelines / Methodology & Instruction) | DOC |
| 4 | Risks and Control Matrix | DOC |
| 5 | Distribution list | PDF, signed by Supervisor |

### Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Supervisor** (head of initiating unit) | Assigns the Responsible Executor; ensures feasibility; confirms compliance with objectives, plan and budget; ensures draft quality and deadline adherence; **defines the minimal list of approvers**; provides clearance |
| **Responsible Executor (Originator)** | Preliminary coordination with stakeholders; formatting and spelling compliance; interaction with approvers; amendments and re-uploads; full package preparation; avoids duplicate approvals within one functional area; **provides justification when rejecting comments** |
| **Reviewers / Approvers** (as a rule, Business Process Owners) | Provide comments as specific items or justified proposals |
| **Document Control** | Verifies compliance; checks attachments, approval sheets and signatures; returns non-compliant drafts to the Initiator |

### Timeframes

Total review and concurrence:
- Organizational and administrative documents — **15 days**
- Local regulations: up to 20 pages (excl. attachments) — **20 days**; 21–40 pages — **25 days**;
  over 40 pages — **30 days**

Maximum per approver:
- **3 days** — ORD and local regulations up to 20 pages
- **5 days** — local regulations 21–40 pages
- **7 days** — local regulations over 40 pages

Finalization to incorporate all amendments: **not more than 3 days**, including for repeated
concurrences. **The number of approval cycles shall not exceed two (2).**

Employees on business trips or vacation remain personally responsible for these deadlines and must
delegate drafts to subordinates for timely review.

### Approval mechanics

- Approvals run **in parallel**, not sequentially.
- **Mandatory approvers cannot be bypassed.** Additional approvers are chosen by the Supervisor by
  subject and kept to a minimum.
- If the Responsible Executor appears on the mandatory approver list, the initiator removes themselves.
- **Silence = approval.** If an approver neither approves nor rejects with substantiated comments by
  the deadline, the draft is deemed APPROVED with the endorsement `NO COMMENTS PROVIDED`.
- **One "Reject" routes the whole draft to Revision.** The system re-routes by default only to those
  who rejected; the Executor may add others. Continuation depends on the Reject decision, not on the
  presence of comments.
- Notes made by approvers **outside their competency** are treated as recommendations only.
- **Refusal to endorse previously approved sections is not allowed.**
- For working-group orders, membership is pre-approved with the relevant business unit managers and
  that approval is attached to the draft.

### Finalization and settlement of differences

The Responsible Executor deletes the previous revision, uploads the revised draft with
`revised as of dd.mm.yyyy` in the filename, recirculates, and attaches a list of explanations for any
partially or fully rejected comments.

On disagreement: the Supervisor arranges a meeting **within one (1) business day** of receiving
comments; the meeting resolution is attached to the draft. If no agreement is reached or deadlines
cannot be met, the draft goes by e-mail to the Director with the comments attached. If the Director
approves, all prior comments are considered resolved; if not, they must be incorporated.

## 6. Confidential documents

Responsibility for assigning the "Confidential" level rests with the drafter, the receiver of
incoming correspondence, the executive signing, or the information resource owner.

- Stamp `CONFIDENTIAL` (EN) / `КОНФИДЕНЦИАЛЬНО` (RU) in the **top right corner** of a one-page
  document or on the cover page. Contracts are exempt from the stamp.
- Drafts are password-protected by the Initiator using the authoring software's security functions.
- Registration requests go to a **dedicated confidential mailbox** — never the common Document
  Control address.
- Stored in PDF, protected by certificate security with an encryption key issued by Corporate Security.
- **Confidentiality duration:** a date or triggering event should be stated (e.g. "until tender
  results announcement", "until December 31, 2026"). Absent a deadline, the mark remains effective
  for **five years** from registration. Commercial secrets may be unlimited.
- Access is limited to persons approved by the Document Initiator; the access list accompanies the
  registration request.
- Transmission through unprotected channels, or forwarding without encryption, is prohibited.
  Electronic transmission only via corporate e-mail with the document password-protected and the
  password communicated **separately**.
- **Never** put passwords in file names, e-mail text, attachments, or on removable media.
- Internally distributed only in electronic form, with **read-only access, copying and printing
  restricted**.
- Hard copies travel in sealed envelopes marked "Confidential", showing the document number, title
  and sender, by courier, with a mailing list specifying recipients and copy counts.
- Where an approval text discloses letter content, the sensitive fragment is removed and replaced
  with `[Part of the text removed, Full Name]` before conversion to PDF.

## 7. Facsimile and digital stamp

- Document Control is the **sole holder** of the Director's facsimile and may not distribute the file
  without the Director's written approval.
- **Each application of the facsimile requires a separate written approval from the Director.** If
  the document changes after approval, re-approval is required, accompanied by a written explanation
  of the changes.
- Documents submitted for facsimile must be **PDF, final and ready to sign**. Native drafts are not
  accepted, to avoid information loss during conversion.
- The request specifies the **page number and location on the page** (e.g. "page 1, top right
  corner") and whether a stamp is required.
- The Director's facsimile is **not** applied to HR documentation (e.g. leave applications).
- **Digital stamp** certifies the authenticity of an executive's signature and of documents
  evidencing legal or financial rights and liabilities, on request with the Director's e-mail
  approval. On financial documents the stamp must **never cover other details**; the place is
  normally marked with the word "Stamp". Wet stamp use remains with Accounting.
- Employees are personally responsible for their own signatures and facsimiles; Document Control is
  not the focal point for anyone's facsimile other than the Director's.

## 8. E-mail

**Out of Office.** Employees absent with limited e-mail access either forward to their back-to-back
or activate an automatic reply stating dates of absence and return and the B2B's contact details.

```
Dear [Sender],
Thank you for your e-mail. I am currently out of the office and will not be able to
respond until [Date of Return].
If your inquiry requires immediate attention, please contact my B2B
[Full Name, E-mail, Phone Number].
For non-urgent matters, I will respond upon my return.
Best regards,
[Your Full Name]
```

**Signature fields, in this sequence:**

| Field | Status |
|---|---|
| Full name | Mandatory |
| Position | Mandatory |
| Company name | Mandatory |
| Mobile phone number | Mandatory |
| Extension number | Optional |
| Office address | Optional |
| Corporate e-mail address | Mandatory |
| B2B (back-to-back) | Mandatory |
| Disclaimer | Mandatory |

**Prohibited in signatures:** logos, photos, hyperlinks, slogans, decorative symbols.

**Formatting:** Calibri or Times New Roman, 11–12 pt (disclaimer 8 pt); black or dark blue;
left-aligned; single line spacing; two spaces separating the office address from the disclaimer.

**Disclaimer** — used without modification:

> DISCLAIMER: The information contained in this e-mail (including any attachments) is intended solely
> for the use of the designated recipient(s) and may contain confidential or privileged information.
> Any unauthorized review, use, disclosure, distribution, or copying, in whole or in part, is
> strictly prohibited and may be unlawful. If you have received this e-mail in error, please notify
> the sender immediately, delete it from your system, and destroy all copies.
>
> This e-mail does not constitute a binding commitment of the Company unless expressly stated and
> confirmed in writing. The Company accepts no liability for any damage caused by possible security
> threats (including viruses or malware). Recipients are responsible for their own protective
> measures.
