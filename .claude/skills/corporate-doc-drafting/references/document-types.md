## 1. Classification tree

```
Correspondence
├── Incoming (electronic / paper)
├── Outgoing (electronic / paper)
└── E-mail
Office Memorandum
├── To the Director
├── Between Business Units
└── For Purchase
Executive documents (ORD)
├── Order on principal activity   — in force until cancelled
├── Order on personnel            — HR-prepared, restricted access
├── Directive                     — limited validity, until executed
└── Minutes of Meeting
Local regulations
├── Procedure      — 2nd-level business process
├── Guidelines     — 3rd (and lower) level
├── Instruction    — 4th (and lower) level; sequences of operations
├── Methodology    — 4th (and lower) level; methods, norms, calculations
└── Operations Technical Documentation (OTD) — operational processes, revision-based
```

The **business-process level determines the document type**. Do not call something a Procedure if it
sits at the third level; that is a Guidelines. Do not call a Guidelines an Instruction. The hierarchy
is: Procedure (goals, responsibility distribution, major decisions, KPIs) → Guidelines (links actions
to information systems, responsibility for execution) → Instruction (sequence of operations,
clarifies the Guidelines) / Methodology (methods, techniques, norms, calculations).

## 2. What makes correspondence "official"

Correspondence is official — and therefore must be registered — if **any one** of these holds:
- it is between the Operator and the NOC;
- it is between the Operator and the parent group;
- it is between the Operator and a representative of government agencies;
- it is between the Operator and a Contractor **and** it: contains instructions or an Operator
  decision; contains an inquiry; affects the work schedule, value of works, or relates to
  non-compliance with contract terms or technical specifications;
- it transmits information that may influence the Operator–Contractor relationship.

**E-mail is uncontrolled** and must never be used to issue official instructions or formal decisions.
If an e-mail acquires official significance (e.g. it may feature in arbitration), it is forwarded to
Document Control **on the day of receipt** and registered like incoming correspondence. The decision
is taken in consultation with the line manager.

## 3. Outgoing letters — general rules

- **Style:** first-person plural or third-person singular — "We submit for consideration…",
  "The Company does not object to…". First-person singular is permitted only in confidential letters
  addressed personally.
- **One heading, one issue.** Each letter addresses a single subject.
- **Two-part structure:**
  1. Grounds — facts, events, or references to other documents.
  2. Conclusions, proposals, requests, or resolutions.
  If multiple requests are made, structure the text into sections and paragraphs.
- **Regulatory content may never be issued as a letter.** A letter may only clarify or explain
  provisions of an existing regulatory document.
- **Supporting materials** justifying issuance must accompany any draft letter submitted for the
  Director's signature.
- **More than four addressees** → prepare a distribution list instead.
- **Dispatch:** either from the Document Control mailbox (preferred) or self-distributed by the
  department after registration, in which case the "self-distribution" box is ticked in the
  registration request and a **sent-confirmation e-mail** is returned to Document Control for
  attachment to the compound. Hand-delivered letters are still registered.
- **A letter sent without a registration number is not official.**
- **Wet-signed letters** are scanned (300 dpi, correctly oriented, legible) and sent to Document
  Control; the Owner and Initiator are responsible for scan quality.

### Attachments record

Listed after the main text, with page counts:
```
Attachments:  1. Manning table … on 4 pages
              2. Cost estimates … on 2 pages
```
If an attachment itself carries attachments, say so:
```
Attachment: Letter No. MT 31 of the Ministry of Finance of Russia dated 28.03.2025
and Attachment thereto, 30 pages in total
```
Attachments containing confidential information carry the confidentiality mark.

## 4. Addressee block

- Upper **left** corner, left-aligned.
- Name typed in lowercase (except initials), **not underlined**.
- Organisation (or business unit) name in the **nominative case**.
- `Attn.:` precedes the name, followed by the form of address — Mr., Mrs., Ms., Miss, Dr., H.E.
- **No quotation marks** around a company name in English. All significant words in position and
  company names are capitalised.
- Unknown recipient → `To Whom It May Concern`.
- Mailing address, if given, follows the entity name.
- **Maximum four recipients.** Do not write "CC" before the 2nd, 3rd or 4th addressee. Beyond four,
  prepare a mailing list. "CC" is used only in English-language documents.

Examples:
```
Attn.: Mr. Smith
Executive Director
National Oil Company
```
```
Attn.: Mr. Abdullah Abbas Bunyan
Director General,
JMC Chairman of the Block 10 Project
Thi-Qar Oil Company
```
```
Oil Marketing Company (SOMO)
Iraq, Baghdad
AL-Hay AL-Mou'tasim – District 724 – Street 17
P.O. Box 5118
```

Multiple units or several positions of one addressee are listed separated by commas after the name.

## 5. Letters to the parent company

- **Only the Director** may sign letters to officials, Group companies, Committees or management
  bodies of the parent group. This authority is exclusive and non-delegable.
- Follow the numeric file-naming convention (see `formatting-spec.md` §7).
- If replying to a prior document, cite its number and date in both the draft and the registration
  request.
- Signature attribute must include job title + handwritten signature + printed name (initials and surname).
- Executor mark in the lower-left corner where required.

### Capitalisation under the parent charter

Capitalised: General meeting of PJSC LUKOIL shareholders; Board of directors; Executive board;
President; Internal audit commission; Independent auditor. Also capitalised: First executive
vice-president; First vice-president; Senior vice-president; Vice-president; Acting President;
Directorate; Business Unit; Administration; Heads of organizations of LUKOIL Group.

Lower-case (Executive Office employees): head of Directorate; head of Business Unit;
head of Administration; head of section.

## 6. Letters to the NOC and state authorities

- Signed by the Director, or by an Asset Official acting on his behalf during absence. Any other
  employee requires a POA issued by the Operator and signed by the Director.
- **Recommended length: not more than two pages.**
- The **"Drafted by" detail is omitted**.
- Prescribed preparation route:
  1. Responsible Executor drafts in English, per the Guidelines and the Business Style Guide.
  2. Circulates by e-mail to concerned employees for review, if necessary.
  3. Legal Division reviews the finalised draft — **unless the Director approves the draft directly**,
     in which case legal review is required only if the Director specifically asks for it.
  4. Responsible Executor submits the reviewed draft with all attachments for the Director's approval.
  5. On approval, the draft goes to the Translator for Arabic translation where applicable.
  6. The draft plus attachments and all approvals (divisions, translator, Director) goes to Document
     Control at the designated address.
  7. Document Control verifies details, attachment completeness, availability of referenced
     documents, correct letterhead and accuracy of addressee names. Non-compliance → returned with
     comments for correction and resubmission. No comments → facsimile applied, stamp if required,
     registration, dispatch.

## 7. Office Memorandums

- Addressed to **one person only**; others may be listed as reading in copy.
- Mandatory details: registration number; date; address; heading; note on attachments (if any);
  signature (position and full name).
- **Consideration period: not more than ten (10) days.**
- Memos to the Director use the official Office Memo template and are signed by Deputy Directors and
  Managers reporting directly to the Director.
- Registered by Document Control **before** submission — bypassing Document Control is prohibited.
- Memos between business units are issued in the DMS by the responsible employees, and must not be
  used for matters resolvable through routine operational interaction.
- **Memos for Purchase** require the Director's approval unless that authority is delegated by POA
  or internal by-laws.

The Director's final resolution on a memo is only ever **APPROVED** or **REJECTED**, recorded on the
title page, top right corner:
```
APPROVED
By e-mail [DD.MM.YYYY]
Full Name
Director
Signature
```

## 8. Orders and Directives

- Drafted in the **first-person singular**.
- **Heading** begins with the preposition "On" and states the content concisely — `On the plan approval …`
- **Recital** states the reasons and purposes of issuance. If the basis is another enactment, give its
  type, reference number, date, title and relevant provisions. A directive may omit the recital.
- **Instruction section opener:** `I HEREBY ORDER` for an order; `I HEREBY OBLIGE` for a directive.
- Every instruction carries a specific task, a deadline, and a responsible Executor. Where the
  timeframe exceeds **three months**, intermediate milestones are mandatory. Dates are numeric.
- Numbered paragraphs and subparagraphs (`1.`, `1.1.`, `1.2.`). Text after the numeral and dot begins
  with a capital letter. **Verbs in the infinitive.** Vague wording prohibited.
- Executor grouping:
  - Same deadline, multiple tasks → name Executor and date once in the main paragraph, tasks in subparagraphs:
    ```
    1. Finance Director (full name) by 15.08.2025:
    1.1. To finalize the forms of reports…
    1.2. To develop the Regulation …
    ```
  - Different deadlines → Executor once, each task with its own deadline in its own subparagraph:
    ```
    1. Operations Director (full name):
    1.1. To develop model forms of contracts by 01.08.2025.
    1.2. To prepare a draft Agreement by 15.08.2025.
    ```
- Independent documents approved by an order (regulations, plans) must be referenced in the text as
  attachments.
- **Issuing a new order on a subject already covered by a previous order is prohibited.** Orders must
  not contradict or duplicate existing ones. Amendments, revisions and cancellations are formalised
  **only through orders**:
  `To amend Order No. 25 dated 10.11.2024…` / `To consider Clause 2 of Order No. 25 dated 10.11.2019 null and void`
- **Never** write "The order shall be communicated to …". Recipients belong in the distribution list.
- The **final paragraph** assigns responsibility for monitoring execution:
  `[Position, Full Name] shall assume responsibility for monitoring execution of this Order`
  or `I assume personal responsibility for control over execution hereof`.
- **Length: not more than two pages**, excluding attachments.
- Attachment header notation, top right corner of every attachment:
  ```
                                                    Attachment
                            to Order No.___/LOIE dated_______.2025
      of Branch of LUKOIL Overseas Iraq Exploration B.V. in Baghdad
  ```
- Attachments may be tabular: titles as nouns in the nominative case, subheadings consistent with
  titles, headings repeated on every page of a multi-page table. Notes marked with superscripts
  (asterisks or numerals), printed at the foot of the page or table, starting with a capital letter
  and ending with a full stop — **maximum three notes per page**.
- Working-group orders present the membership in table form.

**Orders on personnel:** prepared by HR, registered in the DMS by Document Control managers due to
confidentiality, access restricted, communicated to employees against signature, stored by HR.
Distribution list includes Deputy Directors; where a confidential personnel order spans several
Deputy Directors, each receives only the part relating to their own subordinates.

## 9. Minutes of Meeting

- **Definition:** records date, time, location, attendees, subject, agenda, issues discussed and
  agreements reached.
- **Responsibility:** the party initiating the meeting prepares agenda and minutes. Minutes involving
  the Director are prepared by the Meeting Secretary; for other managers, by the designated employee.
- **JMC and its Subcommittees** follow their own Rules and Procedures, which override these rules.
- **Pre-meeting materials:** a summary of not more than five (5) pages; a participants list and
  agenda with full initials and surnames of speakers and co-speakers; the status of previously
  adopted resolutions on the same issues; draft resolutions for each agenda item naming Executors,
  co-Executors and deadlines. Total volume recommended not to exceed ten (10) pages.
- **Date** = the meeting date; multi-day meetings take the last day.
- First page on the established letterhead.
- **Two sections — Introductory and Main.**

Introductory:
```
CHAIRPERSON: [Full Name]
SECRETARY:   [Full Name]
ATTENDEES:   [List of names/titles]
AGENDA:
1. On …
   Speaker: I.V. Petrov
```

Main — the **HEARD / SPOKE / RESOLVED** scheme, per agenda item:
```
HEARD:    1. Speaker: I.V. Petrov — On [subject].
SPOKE:    [Brief remarks by attendees, if any]
RESOLVED:
1.1. To [action] by [deadline]. Executor: [Position, Full Name].
1.2. To [action] by [deadline]. Co-Executor: [Position, Full Name].
(Attachments: [title], [pages])
```
Resolutions are formulated in regulatory style — imperative/infinitive — with Executors and
deadlines. Materials attached for a specific decision are explicitly noted in the RESOLVED text.

- **Conciseness:** brief minutes must still carry, at minimum, participants, decisions, responsible
  people and deadlines.
- **Circulation:** the Secretary sends draft minutes to all attendees within **three business days**
  of the meeting; attendees comment within **three business days** of receipt. Absent comments, the
  minutes are signed. Agreement by e-mail is permitted — mark the minutes `Agreed by e-mail` with
  date and time and retain the confirmation.
- **Registration and distribution:** submitted to Document Control for registration, then distributed
  automatically to participating employees. Third-party participants are served by the Secretary or
  Document Control, in the latter case using addresses supplied by the Secretary.
